\begin{abstract}
This dissertation studies aspect-sentiment classification when a
customer-feedback taxonomy changes between training and evaluation. The model
does not discover unrestricted topics; it receives supplied candidate aspects
and predicts a set of applicable aspect-specific sentiments. A controlled
difficulty ladder moves from singleton unseen-aspect detection to joint
seen/unseen prediction, dual-unseen competition with asymmetric label
descriptions, a secondary rich-guidance contrast, and parent-group holdout.
Strict train-only TF--IDF, frozen E5,
supervised DistilBERT, frozen Qwen, and QLoRA-adapted Qwen are compared through
the same candidate-pair interface. The primary analysis measures within-method
degradation, the effect of frozen minimal label definitions, and the effect of
task adaptation; a separate Level~3 contrast tests rich taxonomy guidance
against minimal definitions. Seen/unseen partitions and paired confidence
intervals support the comparisons.
\end{abstract}

\chapter{Data and Task Formulation}
This chapter will describe FABSA, its twelve hierarchical aspects and three
sentiments, the review-level pair-set output, and the common
review--candidate-aspect--candidate-sentiment claim. It will specify the four
active taxonomy-generalisation levels: supplied-candidate single-unseen LOAO,
generalised single-unseen LOAO, the cyclic dual-unseen
\texttt{NN}/\texttt{DN}/\texttt{ND}/\texttt{DD} crossover, its secondary
\texttt{RR} condition, and parent-group holdout. It will also define the
\texttt{example\_filtered} training rule,
all-row evaluation, strict seen-only calibration, and description provenance.

\chapter{Methods}
This chapter will describe the common candidate-pair construction and the five
core methods: strict train-only TF--IDF, E5-base-v2, the DistilBERT
review--candidate cross-encoder, frozen candidate-pair Qwen, and matched
candidate-pair QLoRA. It will distinguish frozen representation, supervised
pair training, and adapter training, and will document nested seen-only model
selection, threshold transfer, score caching, and leakage audits.

\chapter{Results}
This chapter will first freeze the strict Level~1 reference table and then
report the description-complete difficulty path
\texttt{L1-D $\rightarrow$ L2-D $\rightarrow$ L3-DD $\rightarrow$ L4-D}.
Separate tables will present the Level~3 description crossover and the matched
Frozen-Qwen-to-QLoRA adaptation effect; a compact secondary table will present
\texttt{RR} minus \texttt{DD}. Every main comparison will include
overall, seen, and unseen metrics, false-positive and false-negative
diagnostics, conditional sentiment coverage, runtime, and paired uncertainty.
Historical closed-taxonomy, organisation-shift, Gemini, and routing results
will be used only as compact context where they clarify the development of the
research question.

\chapter{Discussion}
This chapter will interpret how candidate competition, multiple unseen labels,
missing definitions, and parent-group shift affect different model families. It
will distinguish representation gains from task-adaptation gains, discuss
seen--unseen trade-offs, minimal versus rich label-side supervision, and
model-specific description effects, and limit
claims to the uncertainty represented by the registered bootstrap, aspect
units, and seeds.

\chapter{Conclusion}
This chapter will summarise what the controlled difficulty ladder demonstrates
about candidate-label aspect-sentiment classification under taxonomy change and
identify the most defensible extensions, including the deferred compound
organisation-plus-taxonomy shift.
