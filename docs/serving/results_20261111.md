# Retrieval-augmented classification and HTTP service evaluation

This campaign evaluates dynamic few-shot retrieval for aspect–sentiment classification and the operational cost of serving it. The frozen selection retains **fixed demonstrations**. On the historical replay, the preselected dynamic candidate reduces missed aspects but produces substantially more false positives and a lower overall pair F1. The fixed cached service has lower latency and higher throughput in the completed closed-loop comparisons, and lower latency at the shared open-loop arrival rates.

## Configuration and selection

The campaign uses Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 with NF4 quantisation and FP16 computation on an RTX 5050 Laptop GPU with 8 GB memory. The first stage scores Y/N aspect presence using the frozen base model. The second reuses the existing `l2-a01` QLoRA sentiment adapter; it is not retrained in this campaign. There are 12 candidate aspects, with Account access designated as the held-out aspect. This is one deployment fold, not a rerun of the original 12-fold thesis evaluation.

The new demonstration index excludes the held-out aspect and entire training reviews labelled with that aspect. It contains 6,954 eligible training reviews and 76,494 aspect–review pairs. This index exclusion must not be expanded into a claim that all related text was absent from earlier adapter training or base-model pretraining.

All presence methods use 2 positive and 2 negative examples in YYNN order, a 1,024-token prompt budget, the same input truncation policy and the same seen-only threshold-search budget. Sentiment uses the existing 384-token limit, pair microbatches of 4 and a 4,096-token batch budget. Quality comparisons disable prefix caching. Thresholds operate on the saved raw Y-minus-N logit margin, retaining distinctions lost when high probabilities round to 1.

Each demonstration carries its own review, aspect card and Y/N label; that label concerns the demonstration's aspect, not necessarily the query aspect. The original global retrieval selects from the pooled Y and N candidates, preferring different aspects within each class when available among the top 256 candidates. The final variant restricts seen-aspect negatives only; positives remain global. E5 separately encodes review text with `passage:` and the aspect name plus definition with `query:`, sums the unit vectors with equal weight and renormalises, for both query and indexed pairs. Its encoder has a separate 256-token truncation limit. No truncation of current reviews in the first-stage LLM prompts does not imply that E5 encoded every token; E5-side truncation incidence has not been measured in this campaign.

The eight configurations are fixed, random seeds 13/37/73, BM25, E5, RRF and `e5_negative_seen`. The latter changes only the two negative demonstrations for seen aspects to same-aspect negatives. The two directed improvement rounds are complete; historical evaluation does not reopen method or threshold selection.

On 100 development records, seen pair F1 is 64.2045 for fixed and 64.3979 for the final dynamic candidate. The 0.1934-point difference does not meet the registered 0.5-point promotion margin. Fixed remains the primary method; the final dynamic method remains a preselected comparator. Classification thresholds and business-review routing are separate selections.

The final dynamic candidate relative to fixed has overall pair F1 **−3.9104%**, held-out pair F1 **−18.7228%**, presence FP **+50.4225%**, and presence FN **−16.1165%**. Reduced FN is not an overall quality improvement.

The original E5 point estimate, 58.8089, exceeds all three registered random configurations (55.9119–57.4125) while remaining below the current fixed configuration. No new significance comparison against random is asserted. This campaign does not establish that dynamic demonstrations are generally ineffective. Cross-aspect example use, the retrieval representation, the independent encoder token limit and example utility are concrete hypotheses for future development-only investigation, not established causes of the observed loss.

The registered 2,000 paired-record bootstrap draws use seed 20261111. Dynamic-minus-fixed overall pair F1 is −2.3750 points, with a 95% percentile interval of [−4.0104, −0.6842]; the held-out difference is −11.6742 points, interval [−18.3262, −4.4341]. The 957 IDs correspond to 929 nonempty retrieval-normalised texts, and one normalised text overlaps development and replay. These intervals resample records, not duplicate clusters, and are descriptive of this historical population.

E5 and the negative variant have exactly equal raw held-out margins and probabilities for all 957 rows. Their held-out output difference is due to different frozen, seen-calibrated thresholds (15.7875 versus 7.40625), which add four false positives without changing TP/FN. It is not evidence of changed held-out demonstrations.

The frozen primary review route sends all 957 records to manual review: automatic coverage is zero and qualified review-volume reduction is **0%**. The final acceptance rule requires at least 100 automatically accepted records after audit, a one-sided 95% Clopper–Pearson lower bound of at least 95% for exact-set correctness, and oracle-corrected pair F1 of at least 95. Replacing all reviewed outputs with gold gives an oracle F1 of 100 by construction; this is neither model accuracy nor measured human labour savings.

## Deployment correctness

The service retains the reference sentiment microbatch order and shapes: it executes a complete original four-aspect group when any aspect in that group is selected. Companion scoring is included in computation counts. Actual sentiment work can therefore be 0/4/8/12 prompts rather than simply the number of output aspects.

Native checks on four inputs for both methods matched collated inputs, scores and labels. Both methods then passed 100 development HTTP comparisons with exact output-set parity. The actual-model fault gate passed its 13 steps, six paired comparisons and four pre-scoring rejection checks. These are path-correctness checks, not gold accuracy.

Fixed prefix caching can change presence scores, so a separate cache-on HTTP replay of all 957 records was registered and completed. It has 957 successful first attempts and no missing, extra or duplicate records. Overall pair F1 remains 60.7353, but only 951/957 output sets are unchanged. Six records change presence output, and exact-set correctness changes from 203 to 202 records. Held-out pair F1 changes from 62.3529 to 61.9883. This supports an unchanged aggregate result on this replay, not per-record losslessness or an equivalence guarantee.

The public Monzo/Starling review snapshot contains 800 registered records: 200 adaptation, 200 calibration and 400 locked evaluation. All eight methods completed 800/800 input-only predictions, and the original QLoRA comparator completed 800/800 HTTP requests successfully. Ratings and model predictions are not substituted for human gold.

Lengths are 461 records at up to 100 characters, 243 at 101–300, 89 at 301–1,000 and seven above 1,000. No method truncates the current review in first-stage prompts. The shared sentiment grid contains 26 left-truncated prompts out of 9,600, all in the longest group. This common grid is computed once, not eight times. Selected-prompt subsets and actual canonical service group execution have different computation counts.

Under frozen thresholds, fixed/E5/final dynamic predict 1,613/2,178/2,352 aspect-presence assignments respectively. Without reviewed gold, these differences do not establish recall, false positives, accuracy or quality drift.

A later input-only normalisation audit finds one nonempty adaptation/calibration overlap. A source-bound purpose policy excludes the adaptation member from future improvement use while retaining the original annotation and evaluation populations. Five distinct nonempty symbol-only originals normalise to empty strings and are not merged. The policy does not establish a final eligible feedback count; reviewed-release, held-out-aspect and duplicate checks remain necessary.

## Service conditions and results

An independent reserve supplies 66 capacity inputs and 65 benchmark inputs. The two actual service forms are fixed with prefix KV reuse and the final dynamic candidate without that prefix cache. Both use the canonical sentiment grouping. There is no prediction memoisation or cross-request E5 query-embedding cache. Results are for local loopback HTTP with a new connection per request, including response parsing and queueing; they are not enterprise-network measurements.

There are six capacity runs, 24 closed-loop runs, 12 open-loop runs, one dynamic one-hour soak and three dynamic burst cycles. Each model load has three excluded warmups. Closed concurrency settings 1/2/4/8 share a process within each method–repeat block; open rates share their corresponding block. Runs drain between settings. All three burst cycles share one process and drain between cycles; their internal normal/burst/recovery phases are continuous.

Capacity calibration completes 180/180 requests. Median C1 success throughput is 0.77593375 reviews/s for fixed and 0.27842088 for dynamic. The frozen shared arrival rates are 0.13921044 and 0.20881566 reviews/s. They are the same absolute schedules for both methods.

Closed-loop results complete 720/720 requests without failure. Table values are medians of three run-level metrics, not pooled request quantiles.

| Concurrency | Fixed HTTP P95 (s) | Dynamic HTTP P95 (s) | Fixed success/s | Dynamic success/s |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1.79 | 5.40 | 0.745 | 0.272 |
| 2 | 3.61 | 9.63 | 0.743 | 0.274 |
| 4 | 6.91 | 18.82 | 0.747 | 0.273 |
| 8 | 12.49 | 35.92 | 0.755 | 0.271 |

Within each concurrency, the median of the three paired dynamic throughput changes is approximately −64.1% to −64.3%. Increasing concurrency increases waiting with little throughput change in this single-request-worker configuration. Concurrency eight does not mean GPU request batching of eight. No closed-loop queue-P95 aggregate is invented from HTTP timing.

Open-loop results complete 192/192 planned requests without failure. At the lower rate, median run HTTP P95 is 1.7016 s fixed versus 5.4605 s dynamic; at the higher rate, 1.6329 versus 5.4772 s. Successful delivery rates are demand-limited here, not measurements of maximum capacity. The finite 90-second schedules include a request at time zero, yielding 13 and 19 attempts per run, and summary throughput uses actual wall time including drain.

The one-hour dynamic soak completes 502/502 planned requests at the lower common rate, without failures or observed retrieval fallback. HTTP P50/P95/P99 are 3.2018/5.7752/6.7513 seconds. All twelve complete five-minute arrival windows have full delivery; their HTTP P95 ranges from 5.4902 to 6.0262 seconds. The run-level queue P95 is 0.12228 ms. There are 65 repeated inputs and 437 successful repetitions after the first, with no changed final label sets within the observed registered policy. This is repeat consistency, not gold correctness. None of the 502 planned attempts completes within the descriptive two-second scheduled-response budget; no contractual SLA is inferred.

The 720 device-level resource samples show GPU memory from 3,114 to 3,189 MiB; complete-window maxima are 3,188–3,189 MiB. Process-tree RSS ranges from about 755 to 2,034 MiB and is not unique physical memory. These observations do not prove an absence of memory leaks. Instantaneous sampled software-thermal flags are inactive, but the driver's cumulative software-thermal counter increases by 1,777.20825 seconds across the soak interval. The different measurement modes must not be collapsed into a claim of no thermal limiting.

Each burst cycle schedules 21 normal, 17 peak and 26 recovery requests. The peak arrival rate is 0.55684177/s for 30 seconds, after a 145-second normal phase; recovery lasts 180 seconds at the normal rate. All three cycles run in one dynamic server session, with draining between cycles.

| Cycle | Success / planned | HTTP 429 | Recovery confirmed after peak ends (s) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 61 / 64 | 3 | 103.8569 |
| 2 | 63 / 64 | 1 | 96.4293 |
| 3 | 62 / 64 | 2 | 59.7783 |

Each recovery phase still has one rejected arrival, so reducing the offered rate is not itself recovery. The predeclared rule requires three consecutive scheduled recovery requests to succeed with HTTP latency no greater than 1.10 times the same cycle's normal-phase P95, and queue time no greater than max(100 ms, 1.10 times its baseline queue P95). Each cycle has 21 successful baseline requests, above the required ten. Confirmation is measured at completion of the three-request streak. All cycles satisfy this observational rule; it is not a production recovery SLA or a guarantee of permanent recovery.

Across all 46 registered service runs, there are 1,786 planned attempts, 1,780 successes and six failures, all HTTP 429 under burst load. The stability analysis covers all 16 registered open/soak/burst runs. There is no fixed-method soak or burst comparator, so those dynamic-only tests do not establish relative improvement.
